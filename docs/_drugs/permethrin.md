# Permethrin: From Scabies to Trombiculiasis

## One-Sentence Summary

Permethrin is a pyrethroid antiparasitic agent whose established pharmacology targets sodium-channel function in arthropod nervous systems (used clinically against mite infestations such as scabies, *Sarcoptes scabiei*). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Trombiculiasis** (chigger/harvest-mite dermatitis), a mechanistically related mite infestation, but this direction is currently supported by only **1 veterinary case-series publication** and **no clinical trials or human data**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for Permethrin is not available in this Evidence Pack (`original_moa` is flagged as a data gap). Based on known pharmacology referenced in the model's rationale, Permethrin is a pyrethroid that disrupts sodium-channel function in the nervous system of mites and other arthropods, and its efficacy against scabies (caused by the mite *Sarcoptes scabiei*) is well established in clinical practice.

Trombiculiasis is dermatitis caused by chigger mite larvae (e.g. *Neotrombicula autumnalis*), which — like scabies mites — belong to the Acari (mite) family. Because Permethrin's killing mechanism acts on mite neuromuscular sodium channels broadly rather than on a species-specific target, the same mechanism is plausible against chigger mites, giving this prediction reasonable mechanistic grounding.

However, the only supporting evidence currently available is a 2004 veterinary case series in dogs, not human clinical data. The mechanistic plausibility is credible, but translation to a validated human indication has not been demonstrated.

*Note on other TxGNN predictions in this evidence pack:* lower-ranked candidates (lip neoplasm, lower lip fistula, anisakiasis, toxascariasis) were also generated by the model but are assessed in the rationale data as likely knowledge-graph noise — they have no plausible mechanistic link to Permethrin's arthropod-specific sodium-channel activity, and no supporting literature or trials exist for any of them. This report therefore focuses on the strongest candidate, Trombiculiasis.
